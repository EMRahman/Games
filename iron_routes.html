    How to play

    Score the most points by claiming railway routes, linking the cities on your destination tickets, and building the longest continuous line.

    On your turn, do one thing

    • Draw train cards. Take two cards, from the five face-up cards or the face-down deck. A face-up locomotive is wild, but taking one uses your whole turn, and it can't be your second card.
    • Claim a route. Tap a route on the map. Pay as many cards of its colour as it has spaces. Grey routes take any one colour. Locomotives can stand in for any colour.
    • Attempt a tunnel. Tracks with dashed centres are tunnels. Choose your base payment, then reveal up to three cards from the deck. Each card matching your chosen colour and each locomotive adds one card to the cost. If your base payment is all locomotives, only revealed locomotives count and only locomotives cover the extra cost. You can abandon the attempt and keep your payment cards. Either way, the revealed cards go to the discard pile and your turn ends.
    • Build a station. Choose a city without a station. Your first, second and third stations cost one, two and three cards of a single colour respectively. Locomotives are wild.
    • Draw destination tickets. Draw three tickets and keep at least one.

    Scoring

    • Routes score as you claim them: 1, 3, 5, 8, 11 or 15 points for 1 to 6 spaces.
    • At the end, each ticket whose cities are connected adds its value. Each unfinished ticket subtracts its value. A station can borrow one opponent’s track touching its city. The game picks the tracks that give you the best ticket score, using the same track from each station across all of your tickets.
    • Each unbuilt station is worth 3 points. Your own tracks count toward the 12-point bonus for the longest continuous line. Ties all score it.

    Details

    • New games on every map include tunnels and stations.
    • Everyone starts with 40 trains, 4 train cards, and 3 tickets, and keeps at least 2 of the tickets.
    • If three locomotives are ever face up together, all five face-up cards are replaced.
    • Double routes: one player can't claim both tracks. With 2 or 3 players, only one track of each pair can be used.
    • When a player ends a turn with 2 or fewer trains, everyone, including that player, gets one last turn.

    Computer players

    • Easy computers play loosely. Normal computers plan the shortest lines for their tickets. Hard computers also take on more tickets, and grab locomotives when they're short of cards.
    • Computers' cards and tickets stay hidden. Use the speed buttons during their turns.

    Controls

    Drag to pan and pinch, use the mouse wheel, or use the + and − buttons to zoom. Tap a ticket to highlight its cities. You can select tracks from the Routes you can claim list. Choose Build station to pick a city from the map or the city list. Press D to draw from the deck, 1–5 to take a face-up card, T to draw tickets, or Esc to clear a selection. The game saves on this device after every move.
